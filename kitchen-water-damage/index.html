Kitchen Water Damage Help in Round Lake Beach

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Round Lake Beach crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

The Dishwasher Cavity

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
