Common Water Damage Issues in Rockford

Most water damage calls we take in Rockford trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Rockford homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. A lot of the mold we find in Rockford comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
