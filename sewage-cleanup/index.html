Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Round Lake Beach Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Round Lake Beach, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Round Lake Beach

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
