Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Round Lake Beach

Wood floors react to water quickly. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Warning Signs

  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • Planks pulling loose from the subfloor or peaking where they meet
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Wide spaces between planks once the wood dries too quickly
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. After the moisture leaves, each board is left with a raised center, which is crowning.

Mold Under Boards

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. Mold in that layer cannot be seen from the room, and a musty odor is usually the first sign.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Water That Keeps Moving

Water under a floor travels. Cabinets and wall plates do not stop it, so it may enter adjacent rooms or come out at the ceiling one floor down.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

Past one week

Beyond Drying

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Measure the Moisture

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Set Drying Mats

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.
