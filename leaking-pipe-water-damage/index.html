Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Round Lake Beach

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Round Lake Beach finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. This is where our team comes in. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Wet Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Flooring Damage

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the leak lasted days or months, and the size of the area it wet.
  • What the floors and finishes are made of, because certain materials cannot be dried in place.
  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.
  • How many rooms or levels the water reached, including a ceiling below the leak.
