Wet Carpet Drying: What Round Lake Beach Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Round Lake Beach, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Damp Base of the Walls

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
