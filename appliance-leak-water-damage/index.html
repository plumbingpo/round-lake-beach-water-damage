Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Round Lake Beach

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Round Lake Beach crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Kitchen Cabinets

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Adjoining Rooms

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
