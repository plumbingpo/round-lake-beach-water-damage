AC Leak Water Damage in Round Lake Beach: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A missing or failed float switch that should have shut the system off.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboards and Door Trim

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Closet Walls and Platform

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.
